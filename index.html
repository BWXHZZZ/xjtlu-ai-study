Experimental Study Design

How do different modes of AI assistance and prompt-literacy training affect students' academic writing performance, cognitive load, and attitudes toward AI at XJTLU?

Project Overview

Generative AI tools are already part of everyday study life at XJTLU. Our project investigates not whether students use AI, but how AI should be integrated into learning so that it improves outcomes without increasing dependency or cognitive overload.

Q1Do we need a formative study?

Yes. Before running the experiment we will run a small formative study, for three reasons:

  1. Ground the task in reality. A short survey (~80 students) plus 5–6 semi-structured interviews with students and language instructors will tell us which AI tools (e.g. ChatGPT, ERNIE Bot, Copilot) students actually use for academic writing, and for what.
  2. Calibrate task difficulty. A pilot of the writing task with 4–6 students ensures the task is challenging but completable in 25 minutes, and that our rubric produces reliable scores.
  3. Identify confounds early. Prior AI experience and English proficiency vary widely at XJTLU; the formative study tells us what to measure and control for in randomisation.

Q2Participants and tasks

Who

  • 100 XJTLU undergraduate students (Year 1–3), recruited via campus posters and course announcements.
  • Mixed disciplines and both Chinese and international students, reflecting XJTLU's population.
  • Screened for English writing proficiency (self-report + pre-test) and prior AI usage, used as covariates.
  • Randomly assigned to one of five conditions (20 per group).

What they do

  • Main task: write a 300-word argumentative essay in English on a campus-relevant topic (e.g. "Should attendance be mandatory at XJTLU?").
  • Supporting tasks: pre-test of topic knowledge, post-task questionnaires (cognitive load, trust, satisfaction), and a delayed retention quiz one week later.
  • Depending on condition, they work with a free-form AI chatbot, a guided AI tutor, or no AI at all.

Q4Between, within, or mixed?

Fully between-group design with a 2 × 2 factorial structure, giving 4 experimental conditions, plus a fifth no-AI baseline group (see Q6):

  • Why between-group: the writing task has strong learning and carryover effects — a student who has written the essay once cannot "unlearn" it, so within-group comparison is not feasible without changing the task itself.
  • Why factorial: it lets us estimate the interaction — e.g. prompt training may only help with the free-form chatbot, while the guided tutor already embeds good prompting behaviour.
  • Conditions (5 total): C1 free-form + training · C2 free-form + no training · C3 guided tutor + training · C4 guided tutor + no training · C5 no-AI baseline.

Q6What makes a good baseline condition?

A good baseline isolates the effect of the treatment while keeping everything else identical. Ours is the C5 no-AI control group:

  • Same room, same time limit, same essay topic, same computer — but with no AI tool available (standard text editor only).
  • It answers the fundamental policy question: is AI integration better than what we do now? Without this anchor, a difference between C1–C4 would only compare AI tools against each other.
  • To keep it fair, C5 participants also complete the pre-test and all questionnaires, so every measurement is comparable across all five groups.

Q7Experimental procedure and duration

Main session (60 minutes, in a computer lab)

  1. Consent & demographics — 5 min
  2. Pre-test — topic knowledge & argumentation skills, 10 min
  3. Intervention — prompt-literacy training for trained groups (10 min); untrained groups get a neutral filler activity
  4. Writing task — 25 min, with the assigned AI condition (or none)
  5. Post-questionnaires — NASA-TLX, trust, satisfaction — 10 min

Follow-up

  • +1 week: 10-minute online retention quiz to measure lasting learning rather than short-term output.

Timeline

  • Week 0: formative study & pilot · Week 1: all lab sessions · Week 2: follow-up quiz, grading, and analysis. Total ≈ 2 weeks of data collection.
